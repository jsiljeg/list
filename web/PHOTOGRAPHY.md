# Photography brief — Theatrium

For the shoot this week. Written against the pages that already exist, so
every shot below has a slot waiting for it.

The site is **dark** — warm near-black ground, champagne accent. Images that
were lit for a white page will look wrong here. That single fact drives most of
what follows.

## 1. Dishes — the priority

**All 26 live dishes.** These are the pages guests land on from search
("wine with beef wellington"), and a dish page without a photograph is a page
that does not convert.

For each dish:

- **Two angles**: straight overhead (flat-lay) and a low 30–45°. The site uses
  3:2 and 4:3 crops, so shoot loose enough to crop both without losing the
  plate.
- **Dark surface.** Slate, dark wood, dark stone — not white marble. The plate
  should sit on something close to the page's own ground so the image does not
  punch a bright rectangle into a dark layout.
- **One consistent light setup across all 26.** Consistency matters more than
  perfection here: 26 dishes lit 26 ways looks amateur in a grid, and the menu
  index shows them in a grid.
- **Side light with a soft fall-off** reads best on dark backgrounds. Avoid
  flat frontal light — it kills the texture that makes food look edible.
- No garnish that is not on the actual plate served. A guest comparing the
  photo to what arrives is the fastest way to lose trust.

**Shot list** — the live menu, by course:

- *Predjela* (starters) — every dish currently served
- *Juhe* (soups) — soups are the hardest to photograph; give them extra time,
  and shoot at a lower angle than the others so there is something to see
- *Glavna jela* (mains)
- *Deserti* (desserts)

I will send the exact current list of 26 names before the shoot day so nothing
is missed and nothing parked is shot by mistake.

---

## 2. Hero — the room

The front page is one full-bleed image. It needs to say "evening, warm,
theatrical" in a quarter of a second.

- **Evening light, service in progress.** Empty rooms photograph as cold. Even
  a couple of laid tables with candles beats a perfect empty room.
- **Wide, and shot loose.** It gets cropped hard on a phone. Leave room top and
  bottom; the headline sits over the lower third, so keep that area
  uncluttered and slightly darker.
- **Landscape 16:9 and a vertical 4:5 of the same scene** — the phone hero
  needs the vertical, and cropping a landscape to portrait never works.
- Include the atrium and, if it can be done naturally, the **nail sculpture**.
  It is already the mark used across both properties.
- 3–4 genuinely different options, not four frames of one setup.

---

## 3. Chef — Filip Horvat

- **One environmental portrait** in the kitchen or pass, working, not posed
  against a wall. This is the frame press will ask for.
- **One three-quarter portrait**, calmer, usable at small sizes.
- **One horizontal with negative space** on one side — for a page header where
  text sits beside him.
- Shoot in the same warm register as the room. A crisp white-background
  corporate headshot will not fit anywhere on this site.

## 4. Interior details

Eight to ten frames, used as section breaks and card images:

- the nail sculpture, close, raking light across it
- the atrium ceiling / architecture
- a laid table, close, from a guest's eye height
- glassware — the actual stemware, backlit
- hands working: plating, pouring, finishing a dish
- the kitchen in service, motion allowed
- a corner of the room with the blue interior showing
- the bar / bottle shelf

These do not need to be perfect. They need to be *textural* and to carry the
same warmth as the hero.

## Technical requirements

- **RAW plus full-size JPEG.** Do not send only exports.
- **Minimum 2400 px on the long edge**, 3000+ preferred. We downscale; we
  cannot upscale.
- **Consistent white balance** across the set. Colour-correct as a batch.
- **No heavy filters, no vignettes, no baked-in borders.** We handle grading in
  CSS and need clean source.
- **Shoot loose.** Every image gets cropped to at least two aspect ratios
  (16:9, 4:3, 3:2, 1:1, 4:5). Tight framing kills half of those.
- **Consistent orientation per category** — all 26 dishes framed the same way.

## Rights and admin — settle before the shoot, not after

- **Written licence for unlimited web use**, including the site, Google
  Business Profile, social and press distribution. A licence limited to
  "social media" will block the press page later.
- **Model releases** for any staff or guest whose face is identifiable. Guests
  in the background need consent or must not be recognisable — this is GDPR,
  not politeness.
- Agree who holds the RAWs.

---

## What we do with them

Nothing needs resizing by hand. Drop the full-size files in and the build
produces AVIF/WebP at the sizes each layout actually uses, with correct
`width`/`height` so the page does not shift while they load. Send the largest
version of everything.

**Naming:** `dish-<slug>-01.jpg` for dishes using the slug from the menu (I
will send the list), `hero-01.jpg`, `chef-01.jpg`, `interior-01.jpg`,
`exterior-01.jpg`, `wine-01.jpg`. Anything consistent works — I can rename in
bulk — but consistent beats descriptive.
