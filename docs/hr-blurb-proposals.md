# Croatian producer blurbs — proposed reorderings

**Nothing here is applied.** These are proposals for the owner to accept or
reject one by one (2026-08-12). Facts are preserved in every case; what moves
is the order in which a guest meets them, and the odd phrase that has to bend
to let a sentence lead.

Note before accepting: a blurb is one text in eight languages. Reordering the
Croatian means reordering hr, en, it, fr, de, sl, es and zh — the same story in
each, not a translation of the new Croatian.

---

## What we concluded before (from CLAUDE.md, restated)

1. **The blurb owes the guest no facts at all.** Grape, region, body, alcohol,
   temperature, aromas, food and price are all already above it on the card.
   The winery section starts 711 px down a 906 px card: nobody reaches it by
   accident. Its one job is to make the bottle feel worth the money.
2. **The test for what goes in: would a guest say this out loud to the person
   across the table?** "Osnovano 1597." — nobody has ever said that to anyone.
   "Odbio je prodati boce kraljici jer je auto bio bez klime" — everybody says
   that.
3. **One story, one date.** A second date only when it is the *payoff* of the
   first. Four or more distinct years is a CV, and a CV is tiring at any
   length. Quintarelli — the blurb the owner liked best — is 577 characters
   and names one year.
4. **Erudition earns its place only as the setup for a payoff.** Research
   everything, print the payoff.
5. **The shape the best cards share:** a person, a decision that cost them
   something, and a line that lands. Never founded → generations → hectares →
   certifications.
6. **Figures, not words:** `25 godina`, `1.700 maslina`, `12.000 €`.

The Croatian set was reviewed against this on 2026-08-12: **34 estates, of
which 24 open well and are left alone.** Ten open on an address, a founding
date, a hectare count or an etymology while the line that would make a guest
keep reading sits in third place.

---

## 1. Jakopić — the strongest case

Opens on a manor and 1908; the thing worth repeating is the last clause of the
last sentence. Four facts compete before the guest gets there.

**Now**

> Kurija Zichy-Terbócz stoji na Železnoj gori, a obitelj Jakopić vino tu radi
> od 1908. Restoran su otvorili 2002., kuriju obnovili u baštinski hotel, i
> vino po njoj nosi ime. Branimir i Nada postavili su temelje, sinovi Filip,
> Martin i Vinko preuzimaju; oko 15 hektara graševine, sauvignona i pušipela —
> imena kojim Međimurje zove furmint i koje je samo vratilo iz zaborava.

**Proposed** (340 chars, one date)

> Pušipel — tako Međimurje zove furmint, i sámo ga je vratilo iz zaborava.
> Jakopići ga rade na Železnoj gori, uz graševinu i sauvignon, na 15 hektara
> oko kurije Zichy-Terbócz; kuriju su obnovili u baštinski hotel i vino po njoj
> nosi ime. U obitelji se vino radi od 1908., a Branimir i Nada danas ga
> predaju sinovima Filipu, Martinu i Vinku.

*Dropped: the restaurant's 2002, a date with no payoff.*

## 2. Saints Hills — the joke is buried in third place

The Frenchie back label is the funniest thing on the Croatian shelf and it is
the last thing the guest reads.

**Now** — opens: "3 vinograda nazvana po djeci obitelji Tolj…"

**Proposed** (500 chars; two dates, the second the payoff of the first)

> Sauvignon Frenchie nosi na etiketi karikaturu Michela Rollanda, a na
> poleđini posvetu „svoj rollandizaciji, globalizaciji, parkerizaciji i ostalim
> glupostima”. Rolland ih je savjetovao od 2006. — jedina hrvatska adresa
> velikog Bordelaisa — i bio im je, kažu, prije svega prijatelj i učitelj:
> savjetovao je, a Ernest Tolj je odlučivao. Vinogradi nose imena djece obitelji
> Tolj: Sveta Lucija, Sveti Roko, Sveti Ante, u Istri i na Komarni. Rolland je
> umro u ožujku 2026.; šala ga je nadživjela.

*The quote is kept verbatim, including „svoj".*

## 3. Niko Bura — the contrast is the story, and it is stated flat

585 years of winemaking and the first bottle under their own name in 1995. As
written, the date leads and the contrast never lands.

**Now**

> 16 naraštaja Bura radi vino na Pelješcu — od 1410. Niko je prvu berbu pod
> obiteljskom etiketom napravio tek 1995., 4.000 boca. …

**Proposed** (300 chars)

> Bure rade vino na Pelješcu od 1410., a prvu bocu s vlastitom etiketom
> napravili su 1995. — 585 godina i 4.000 boca poslije. Šesnaesti je naraštaj
> u kući. Na ovoj karti stoji ono najslađe što rade: Ruža Dalmatinska, prošek
> od rukatca, vino kojim se ovdje oduvijek dočekivao gost kojeg se htjelo
> počastiti.

## 4. Erdoro — etymology first, cross-reference last

The name's derivation is textbook. The claim and the cross-reference are what
a guest repeats — and CLAUDE.md wants cross-references named, because a guest
who spots the connection remembers both cards.

**Proposed** (430 chars)

> Sauvignon koji je ovdje napravio Milan Budinski hrvatska je kritika nazvala
> jednim od najboljih u desetljeću — a Budinskijevo vlastito vino, OMO, također
> je na ovoj karti. Vinograde vodi Francuz Eric Moro, isti čovjek koji savjetuje
> Saints Hills. Ime je spoj dvaju: Erdödy, plemićka obitelj koja je vino na
> Plešivici počela prodavati 1736., i oro — zlato. Danas je Erdoro najveći
> vinogradarski projekt Plešivice.

## 5. Rizman — a nickname opens, a remarkable fact waits

Komarna being an appellation where **every** vineyard is certified organic is
the fact a guest would repeat. It is currently a subordinate clause.

**Proposed** (400 chars; also fixes "tisuću sedamsto" → "1.700", per the
figures rule)

> Komarna je apelacija u kojoj je svaki vinograd certificirano ekološki — svih
> 90 hektara, bez iznimke — a Rizman je 2006. krenuo na goloj zemlji i pomogao
> da tako bude. Rizman je nadimak prvog vinara u obitelji; 5 naraštaja poslije
> to je ime na etiketi, ali vinograd je nov: 22 hektara loze, 1.700 maslina,
> podrum iz 2012. Savjetuje ih Dirceu Vianna Junior, Master of Wine. Kušaonica
> gleda na Pelješki most.

## 6. Geržinić — a straight swap of the first two sentences

Hectares and a grape list open; two brothers and Marks & Spencer wait.

**Proposed** (310 chars, nothing else touched)

> Marko radi vino, mlađi brat Marino etikete i tržišta — i odveo ih je sve do
> polica Marks & Spencera. 10 hektara nad Vižinadom, na položajima kroz koje
> uvijek nešto struji: malvazija, teran, žuti muškat. Uz vino kuća radi i
> maslinovo ulje i, odnedavno, rakiju: u Istri to nije diverzifikacija nego
> opis obitelji.

## 7. Baraka — a straight swap, and the shortest blurb on the list

**Proposed** (225 chars)

> Plavina, babić i lasina drugdje su ispale iz mode; ovdje nisu ukras nego
> glavni posao. Mala šibenska kuća: 4 hektara, 11.500 trsova, suhozidi stari
> stoljeće i serije koje se daju izbrojati.

## 8. Šember — the rare variety should lead, not the place name

**Proposed** (330 chars)

> Plavec žuti gotovo nitko više ne sadi, a za pjenušac je idealan: Šemberima
> drži kiselinu u vinima koja su više od dvije trećine svega što rade, sve
> klasična metoda. Pavel je i selo i crkva — žive u Donjim Pavlovčanima, a nad
> vinogradima stoji crkvica svetog Pavla. U kući je peta generacija.

## 9. Violić — the point is in sentence two

**Proposed** (335 chars)

> Vino se zove po svom vinogradu, a ne po apelaciji, što je na Pelješcu
> rijetkost: Sagul je latinsko ime položaja koji svi zovu Zaguine, na kunovskoj
> strani, jedno od najosunčanijih mjesta na poluotoku. Plavac je srce kuće i
> penje se u tri stupnja, od ovoga do Dingača; uz njega idu rukatac i pošip, u
> bijelom i narančastom.

## 10. Korak — an administrative roll-call opens a card with a Michelin star in it

The weakest case of the ten; take it or leave it.

**Proposed** (400 chars)

> Bernardov restoran u obiteljskoj kući nosi Michelinovu zvjezdicu i zelenu uz
> nju; brat Josip u istoj kući radi vino, sestra Vera vodi kuću. Otac Velimir
> proglašen je 2008. najboljim hrvatskim vinarom. 5 hektara na najboljim
> plešivičkim položajima daje mirna, pjenušava i narančasta vina; Sauvignon,
> Chardonnay i Pinot crni odavde zadrže kiselinu kakvu jug ne može dati.

*Dropped: the house "iz 1900.", a date with no payoff.*

## Left alone, and why

These already open the way the rule asks — a person, an image, or a paradox —
and should not be touched:

- **Clai** — 25 years of a restaurant in Trieste, then health sent him home. A
  decision that cost something, in the first line.
- **Ivanić** — "Punker iz Samobora… bez naslijeđenog trsa, znanja i
  tehnologije."
- **Benmosche** — the man who took AIG out of the largest state bailout in
  history, and the payoff: the Zinfandel he brought was not imported but
  returned.
- **Miloš** — wine out of the stone of Ponikve, and Garmaz unable to decide
  between genius and Don Quixote.
- **Kunjas** — Pošip grows best in two places on earth, and they are in one.
- **Markus** — the best Plavac in the house may not print the name of the
  vineyard its fruit comes from.
- **Krajančić** — writes poems, and does not like being told he whispers to
  the wine.
- **Bire** — Grk cannot pollinate itself, so Plavac is planted between the
  rows.
- **Čečavac** — "Daniel Čečavac nema vinograd."
- **Skaramuča** — the Dingač terraces reached by donkey until the tunnel was
  driven through the hill.
- **Aura** — "Sve se ovdje vrti oko ugljena."
- **Boškinac**, **Meneghetti**, **Benvenuti**, **Tomac**, **Tomaz**,
  **Matošević**, **Mrgudić**, **Petrač**, **Bastian**, **Wise Grus**,
  **Zmajska**, **Čečavac & Gašpar**, **Budinski** — all open on a person or a
  turn, none on a founding date.
